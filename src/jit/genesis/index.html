The launch game rewards concentrated kill zones, complementary jobs, and emergency tools more than carpeting the map with mediocre towers.

S

Default core

Archer Garrison · Knights Order · Scholar Mage · Royal Catapult

Cheap coverage, stall, armor answer, clump answer. The fifth slot solves the map rather than duplicating a job.

Fifth-slot instinct

Ironbark Treant for flexible block/artillery; Dwarven Culverin for compact kill pockets; Elite Ranger for priority targets/flyers.

Why you feel poor

Genesis gives you many attractive purchases while your defense still needs time-on-target. Buying another weak pad often feels productive but dilutes gold that could make the existing choke actually lethal.

Meta confidence

Low-to-medium. The game is days old. Sources disagree sharply on several specialists. Treat consensus on roles as stronger evidence than letter grades.

One sentence: hold enemies where several towers can hurt them, upgrade that pocket until it works, and keep a spell/hero available for the leak you didn't budget for.

Tier letters are less useful than knowing what problem earns a pad.

Placement is an economy multiplier. A tower covering twice as much useful path is effectively cheaper.

Best pad

Shared path + bend + choke + multiple tower overlap. Spend premium gold where geometry multiplies it.

Trap pad

A beautiful isolated pad that only fires briefly at one lane. Range on the stat card is not the same as useful path coverage.

Exit insurance

Don't overbuild the exit. Keep heroes, Reinforcements or Teleportation available instead; static insurance is expensive.

Treat gold as concentrated time-on-target, not permission to fill empty pads.

1 · Establish

Buy the minimum set of jobs that prevents obvious leaks: coverage + a real stall point + the damage type the wave demands.

2 · Deepen

Upgrade towers that already cover the choke before opening another mediocre firing position. Capstone/skill value makes commitment matter.

3 · Specialize

Only then buy the fifth-role answer: armor strip, extra AoE, economy, support, anti-flyer, or a second pocket.

Don't panic-carpet

A leak tempts you to spend everywhere. First ask whether moving a hero/rally point or holding one cooldown fixes the actual failure for free.

Gold-starvation diagnostic: if many pads are occupied but nothing feels strong, stop expanding. If one pocket is strong but a specific enemy escapes it, buy the counter rather than more generic DPS.

Pick a pair with different jobs. Current lists disagree on exact order; Drakkan, Ashbite and Ignus repeatedly appear among strong launch choices.

Pairing rule

One hero stabilizes a lane; the other solves changing pressure. Two heroes doing the same job leave you paying towers/spells to cover the missing one.

Three slots should solve three different kinds of emergency.

S

Reinforcements

Time. Emergency stall, leak catch, temporary frontline. Early consensus is unusually strong here.

S/A

Rain of Fire

Delete. Use on a wave whose removal prevents a collapse, not merely because the cooldown is ready.

A

Royal Edict

Amplify. General-purpose third slot while learning the game; swap when the map exposes a more specific failure.

Situational swaps

Teleportation Sigil buys another pass through your damage; Wintersong's Wrath is cited as strong late crowd control. Avoid three buttons that all solve “deal damage now.”

The launch upgrade system is still being documented. Spend toward the family you actually rely on, not an imagined permanent meta.

Marksman

High early utility because cheap ranged coverage and flyers recur. Good first investment if Archer Garrison is your default opener.

Barrack

Stall converts every nearby tower into more damage. If leaks are your problem, improving the thing that creates firing time can outperform another damage bonus.

Mage

Armor makes magic coverage non-negotiable on the stages that care about it. Invest when Scholar Mage is living in every loadout.

Artillery

Strong when your geometry creates clumps. Less urgent if the campaign segment is mostly sparse durable targets.

Don't optimize stars around launch-week tier letters. Optimize around your repeated loadout. Rebalance risk is exactly why this atlas emphasizes jobs and geometry.
